# Canonical Art Direction

## Overall look
High-end illustrated 2D game art with a bright toy/storybook feel. Top-down or lightly angled top-down environments should remain readable at phone size.

## Character treatment
- Rounded, expressive silhouettes.
- Strong faces and readable poses.
- Saturated rainbow/pastel accents.
- Avoid tiny detail that disappears at gameplay scale.

## Unicorn
White body, rainbow mane/tail, golden horn. The main unicorn should always have stronger visual priority than environmental decoration.

## Baby unicorns
Same family resemblance, smaller body, pastel accent variants. Motion must include visible bounce/trot rather than static follower stickers.

## Halloween Dumplings
Inspiration: cute squishy seasonal dumpling toys. Treat them as original game characters:
- round/soft,
- squash-and-stretch,
- tiny feet,
- playful angry/dizzy/captured expressions,
- spooky-cute motifs.

Do not copy any retail toy package, logo, exact face design, or branded product identity.

## Magical secret doors
Doors should look materially part of the world, then become magical:
- vines / stone / wood / purple arch,
- star or moon motif,
- staged glow,
- open portal interior,
- sparkles and path-light effect.

## Secret rooms
Secret spaces should feel richer than the ordinary level:
- treasure glow,
- candles / crystals / stars,
- magical wall treatment,
- clearly visible exit portal,
- baby unicorn focal point.

## Halloween police map
Whimsical festival / toy-horror energy:
- deep indigo/purple night,
- orange pumpkin lighting,
- candy stands,
- lanterns,
- pumpkins,
- friendly ghosts,
- open chase lanes.

The reference to classic 16-bit top-down action games is about **readability, busy playful maps, and arcade pacing**—not copying copyrighted levels, sprites, characters, audio, names, or layouts.
